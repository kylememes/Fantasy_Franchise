# Weekly seasons and authoritative football

The inaugural season uses the checked-in 2026 NFL preseason (Weeks 1–3, excluding the separate Hall of Fame exhibition) and regular-season schedule. Schedule facts are imported from official NFL weekly pages and validated for unique weekly participation, 17 regular-season games and one bye per franchise. Real scores are never imported.

Draft completion exposes a staging dashboard at Preseason Week 1. The commissioner explicitly opens the week. CPU-only games enter a durable queue immediately. An owner starts a CPU matchup by joining and readying; two human owners must both ready for their matchup. Readying may be cancelled before kickoff. Kickoff atomically freezes effective depth charts, player profiles, saved philosophies, staff names, and a private deterministic seed.

A pure, versioned TypeScript football engine produces a complete event timeline and statistics outside the browser. A Supabase Edge worker claims leased database jobs, computes the engine output, and commits only with its current lease. Expired leases are retryable using the original inputs and seed. Private job/results tables have no client access. A scheduled database dispatcher uses short-lived, single-use worker tickets, so no credentials are placed in source or sent to browsers. Authenticated league members can also wake their league's worker.

CPU games finalize immediately. Human games reveal saved events at a server-owned play cursor; elapsed server time drives live progression. Authorized participants can pause, resume, change speed, step to the next play, or simulate to the end. Polling cannot reroll results. Disconnects preserve the cursor. Completed games expose the immutable timeline and box score; each viewer independently controls replay speed and position. The renderer interpolates the actual engine's routes, rushes, ball flight, pursuit and ending location. It never resolves a play.

Week controls lock the league and check the expected phase/week. Advance requires every current game to be final. Simulate Unfinished queues unstarted games and finalizes prepared live games, without silently advancing. Repeated or racing operations cannot duplicate games, jobs, scores or advancement. Week 3 transitions to Regular Season Week 1; Week 18 ends this regular-season release. Playoff/offseason systems are a subsequent phase, not invented results.

Standings and player statistics are derived from immutable game results, with preseason separate. Head coaches have two explicitly game-assigned specialties (+2 and +1) from data/head-coach-bonuses.json; coordinators remain cosmetic. Effective attributes cap at 99 and never rewrite stored player profiles. New game inputs freeze the coach name, boosted attributes and base/bonus/effective values. Legacy frozen inputs retain the exact V1 calculation; the coach-aware V2 engine uses safety coverage help, defensive strength, tackling, linebacker recognition, tight-end blocking and contested catching. There is one employment per coach per league and one person per staff slot. Human owners can hire first; CPU head-coach vacancies fill atomically from the remaining pool before games queue at Preseason Week 2. Repeated opens cannot duplicate or replace hires. Owner tendencies stay unchanged. Owner settings remain editable for future games, but cannot alter a game's frozen kickoff inputs.

Verification covers schedule invariants, deterministic replay, 22 unique players, saved starter usage, coaching and matchup effects, ensemble statistical ranges, clock/field/scoring invariants, authorization, two-owner readiness, single-use tickets, job leases, private results, final immutability, and racing commissioner advancement.

Hosted acceptance used two independent authenticated clients and a disposable league. Invitation joining, readiness, background CPU games, private results, shared pause/rejoin, immutable finals, staff exclusivity, and racing advancement passed. Test accounts and league were removed; existing draft picks were unchanged. Browser review covered the animated field, live controls, instant replay, box scores, and a 390px-wide viewport using a synthetic roster. The temporary visual review route was removed before release.

## Gameplay V3 and vertical broadcast

New kickoffs freeze `gameplay_version: 3` on both teams. The worker supports V1, V2 and V3; commit validation requires the engine matching the frozen job. Previously queued jobs and completed games are never upgraded or re-simulated. V1 and V2 regression hashes protect exact legacy results.

V3 adds seeded screens, mesh crossers, spacing, flood, verticals, play action, RPOs and occasional flea-flicker concepts; draw, stretch, counter and zone runs use matchup modifiers. Speed, route skill, accuracy, protection, blocking, pursuit and tackling affect resolution. Broken tackles add recorded yards. Fatigue builds during snaps, recovers on the sideline and at stoppages, reduces effective attributes, and selects relief backs and defensive linemen. Fatigue never edits a saved base profile. Situational logic includes the existing down/distance/field-position/score tendencies, late fourth-down and field-goal choices, hurry-up, halftime clock preservation, offensive/defensive timeouts, spikes and victory kneels.

Each V3 event saves formation/personnel, before/after scores and timeouts, participant identities, fatigue, commentary, possession changes and timed paths. The renderer uses the same timeline times for ball and player interpolation. Catch/interception points, tackle/sack participants, loose-ball recovery and touchdown endpoints come from that event. Man defenders follow assigned routes; zone defenders occupy areas with safety help; blockers engage the front and defenders pursue the carrier. This is a schematic 2D replay of a probabilistic football simulation, not a collision-physics engine. Formations use 11 personnel; the formation caption describes that saved alignment rather than promising every NFL personnel package. The displayed play clock is presentation timing, not a separate delay-of-game rules engine.

The canvas is vertical at every viewport, with a default ball-following camera, full-field view, optional names/routes/energy, numbered team-colored circles and a gold possession halo. A live situation strip, formation caption and result banner surround the field. Scores update at resolution. Normal viewing uses the existing four-second server pace. Independent saved/instant replays support quarter-speed, half-speed, normal, 2x and 4x, pause, restart/previous play and next drive. Shared live next-drive control advances only the authoritative cursor to the next possession (or second-half kickoff); owners cannot modify the immutable outcome. Local replay never calls a game mutation.

V3 verification adds synchronized ball-carrier sampling during plays, exact tackle/recovery endpoints, bounded geometry/camera, substitutions, varied calls/coverages, ensemble ranges, frozen V2 regression, pause/resume/slow/restart and reduced-motion checks, plus authorized server drive skipping and wrong-version rejection.
